# Blended Pitch Research

Research notes for a 3-minute judge/demo presentation about Blended: a live, event-sourced learning session layer for teacher-controlled resources, real-time student participation, AI question triage, cursor voting, and replayable class artifacts.

## Core thesis

Blended should be pitched as a way to turn passive, hard-to-read classroom sessions into active, measurable, replayable learning experiences.

The evidence-backed story:

1. Student engagement drops sharply as learners get older.
2. Passive lecture underperforms active learning.
3. Blended learning can outperform purely face-to-face instruction when it adds interaction and structure.
4. Real-time response systems increase participation and feedback loops.
5. Teachers are overloaded; the product has to reduce live-session orchestration burden.

## Deck-ready evidence

### 1. Active learning improves outcomes

**Source:** Freeman et al., “Active learning increases student performance in science, engineering, and mathematics,” PNAS, 2014.  
**Finding:** Meta-analysis of 225 studies found active learning improved exam/concept-inventory performance by about 0.47 standard deviations. Average failure rates were 33.8% under traditional lecturing vs. 21.8% with active learning. Students in traditional lecture were about 1.5x more likely to fail.  
**Use in pitch:** “This is not just engagement theater. Active learning has measurable outcome effects.”  
**URL:** https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1319030111  
**Open access mirror:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4060654/

### 2. Interactive engagement roughly doubled learning gains in a classic physics study

**Source:** Hake, “Interactive-engagement versus traditional methods: A six-thousand-student survey of mechanics test data for introductory physics courses,” American Journal of Physics, 1998.  
**Finding:** Survey of 62 introductory physics courses with 6,542 students found traditional courses averaged normalized gain g = 0.23 ± 0.04, while interactive-engagement courses averaged g = 0.48 ± 0.14.  
**Use in pitch:** “When students are actively responding instead of passively watching, learning gains can change dramatically.”  
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1119/1.18809

### 3. Blended learning can outperform face-to-face instruction

**Source:** U.S. Department of Education, “Evaluation of Evidence-Based Practices in Online Learning: A Meta-Analysis and Review of Online Learning Studies,” 2010.  
**Finding:** Instruction combining online and face-to-face elements showed a larger advantage over purely face-to-face instruction than fully online instruction did. Blended vs. face-to-face mean effect size was +0.35, p < .001.  
**Use in pitch:** “The promise is not online instead of classroom. It is digital interaction strengthening the live classroom.”  
**URL:** https://www2.ed.gov/rschstat/eval/tech/evidence-based-practices/finalreport.pdf

### 4. K–12 blended learning meta-analysis found significant performance gains

**Source:** Zheng et al., “Effect of blended learning on student performance in K-12 settings: A meta-analysis,” Journal of Computer Assisted Learning, 2022.  
**Finding:** Meta-analysis of 84 studies involving 30,377 K–12 students found blended learning significantly improved overall student performance, Hedges’ g = 0.65, p < .001.  
**Use in pitch:** “There is K–12 evidence that blended learning can improve outcomes when implemented well.”  
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1111/jcal.12696

### 5. Student engagement falls sharply with grade level

**Source:** Gallup Student Poll / Gallup education reporting.  
**Finding:** Gallup reports that engagement is much higher among elementary students and drops sharply by high school; one Gallup report framed student engagement as 47% engaged, 29% not engaged, and 24% actively disengaged, with high school engagement around one-third in related Gallup Student Poll reporting.  
**Use in pitch:** “By the time students reach the classrooms where content gets harder, many have already learned to disappear.”  
**URL:** https://www.gallup.com/education/244022/school-engagement-just-talk.aspx  
**Related:** https://www.gallup.com/education/231720/school-engagement-drops-grade.aspx  
**Confidence note:** Strong for pitch-level pain framing; avoid over-claiming causal interpretation.

### 6. Classroom response systems support participation and feedback

**Source:** Kay and LeSage, “Examining the benefits and challenges of using audience response systems: A review of the literature,” Computers & Education, 2009.  
**Finding:** Review identified benefits of audience response systems including participation, engagement, attendance, interaction, immediate feedback, and formative assessment.  
**Use in pitch:** “Clickers proved the appetite for low-friction classroom participation; Blended makes that participation contextual and replayable.”  
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2009.05.001

### 7. Clicker-style tools can increase participation versus hand-raising

**Source:** Stowell and Nelson, “Benefits of Electronic Audience Response Systems on Student Participation, Learning, and Emotion,” Teaching of Psychology, 2007.  
**Finding:** In simulated psychology lectures, clicker groups showed the highest classroom participation and were more likely to respond honestly than students raising hands.  
**Use in pitch:** “Anonymity and low-friction response channels draw out students who would never raise their hands.”  
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1080/00986280701700391

### 8. Formative assessment has large learning effects

**Source:** Black and Wiliam, “Inside the Black Box: Raising Standards Through Classroom Assessment,” Phi Delta Kappan, 1998 / reprint DOI 2010.  
**Finding:** Foundational review argues formative assessment can produce substantial achievement gains; commonly cited effect sizes are around 0.4–0.7 across formative assessment research.  
**Use in pitch:** “The highest-value moment is not after the test. It is while the teacher can still adjust the lesson.”  
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1177/003172171009200119

### 9. Teachers are overloaded and need tools that reduce orchestration burden

**Source:** McKinsey, “How artificial intelligence will impact K–12 teachers,” 2020.  
**Finding:** Teachers work about 50 hours per week and spend less than half that time in direct student interaction; McKinsey estimated AI/automation could save teachers about 13 hours per week by reducing preparation, administration, evaluation, and feedback burden.  
**Use in pitch:** “Blended should not be another place teachers have to manage. It should be the copilot that keeps the live room readable.”  
**URL:** https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/education/our-insights/how-artificial-intelligence-will-impact-k-12-teachers

### 10. Blended/hybrid digital infrastructure is now mainstream

**Source:** NCES Condition of Education, Distance Education Participation, 2023.  
**Finding:** In fall 2021, 9.4 million undergraduates, or 61%, were enrolled in at least one distance education course; 4.4 million, or 28%, took distance education courses exclusively.  
**Use in pitch:** “Digital learning is no longer a pandemic exception; the question is whether live teaching gets better because of it.”  
**URL:** https://nces.ed.gov/programs/coe/indicator/cha/postsecondary-students-distance-education

### 11. Most U.S. students have internet access, but equity still matters

**Source:** NCES Condition of Education, Children’s Internet Access at Home, 2023.  
**Finding:** In 2021, 97% of U.S. 3- to 18-year-olds had home internet access; 93% had access through a computer, while 4% relied only on a smartphone.  
**Use in pitch:** “A browser-first, mobile-responsive approach meets students where they already are — while still designing for access constraints.”  
**URL:** https://nces.ed.gov/programs/coe/indicator/cch

### 12. Edtech market is large, but the pitch should not lean too hard on TAM

**Source:** HolonIQ, “Global Education Technology Market to Reach $404B by 2025,” 2021.  
**Finding:** HolonIQ projected global edtech expenditure to reach $404B by 2025.  
**Use in pitch:** “The market is large, but the stronger wedge is not generic edtech spend; it is live classroom engagement becoming a measurable software layer.”  
**URL:** https://www.holoniq.com/notes/global-education-technology-market-to-reach-404b-by-2025

## Strongest narrative for judges

### Pain

Classrooms are full of invisible confusion. In a normal lesson, the teacher sees the students who raise their hands, the loudest voices in chat, and maybe a quiz score after the moment has passed. Everyone else can disappear.

### Promise

Blended turns the live session itself into an interactive learning surface. The teacher still controls the lesson, but students can respond, ask, upvote, and point to confusion in real time. AI groups the signal so the teacher sees what matters. After class, the session becomes a replayable learning artifact.

### Wedge

Do not pitch it as “Zoom for classrooms” or “another LMS.” Pitch it as:

> The live learning session layer: teacher-controlled resources, real-time participation, AI question triage, and replayable event history.

## Best stats to use in a 3-minute pitch

Use only 3–4 numbers verbally:

1. Active learning reduced failure rates from 33.8% to 21.8% in a 225-study PNAS meta-analysis.
2. Blended learning showed a +0.35 effect over face-to-face instruction in a U.S. Department of Education meta-analysis.
3. K–12 blended learning meta-analysis across 30,377 students found significant performance gains, Hedges’ g = 0.65.
4. Gallup reports only about half of students are engaged, with engagement dropping sharply by high school.

## Caveats

- Avoid implying Blended itself has proven outcomes before pilot data exists.
- Frame evidence as support for the underlying mechanisms: active learning, real-time feedback, formative assessment, and blended interaction.
- Avoid over-indexing on edtech TAM; judges will respond more to the concrete classroom pain and demo credibility.
